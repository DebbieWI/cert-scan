## What you are building

A free, public table showing, for every U.S. state, whether the people licensed to run drinking water and sewage treatment plants must learn cybersecurity to get or keep their license, with the exact rule behind every answer.

## Why it matters

Water plants are run by computers that can be hacked. The people at the controls are licensed by their state, and each state decides what that license requires. Nobody keeps a current list of which states now require cybersecurity training. A regulator, a trainer, or a water utility in one state cannot easily see what the other forty-nine are doing. CERT-SCAN is that list, kept up to date every year.

Think of it as a report card for state licensing rules, where every grade comes with a footnote to the law.

## Where the information comes from

All sources are free and public.

| Source | In plain terms | What one entry looks like |
|---|---|---|
| EPA 816-R-16-002 (March 2016) | The last time EPA summarized all fifty states' licensing programs | Two pages per state: license classes, training, renewal rules |
| State rules (codes and registers) | Each state's written licensing law | One section, such as "10 NYCRR Appendix 5-E section 5-E.7" |
| State board pages, forms, and minutes | Where a state announces a requirement that is not yet in its written rules | A renewal form checkbox, a board notice |
